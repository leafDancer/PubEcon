## Interpretation

- Crossref is the M1 source of record. It covers AEA deposits from 1999 onward; the pre-1999 archive is out of scope by decision.
- `content_type` separates research articles from front matter, issue metadata, unattributed records, errata, AEA association reports/minutes, retraction notices, and duplicates. Only research articles feed the year-level and network statistics.
- Duplicates are detected with a punctuation-insensitive title key, normalised issue number, page-range overlap (roman numerals included), and author-set similarity. The canonical record keeps the modern `10.1257/aer...` DOI where possible; other rows are marked `content_type='duplicate'` with a `duplicate_of_paper_id` pointer.
- P&P articles are identified as the largest spring issue in each year through 2017. The old issue-number heuristic was wrong because the issue number moved from 2 to 3 to 5 over time.
- Affiliation strings are publish-time bylines. Coverage is high in recent years and weaker in the early 2000s; missing values are left missing rather than imputed.
- Rank extraction is a regex over the byline and is intentionally conservative. Any rank/title mention covers 6.2% of slots, but explicit academic rank covers only 0.3%; 5.9% are non-academic titles. Academic byline rank cannot support career inference on its own.
- Canonical author counts use middle-initial-, particle-, and Unicode-aware matching. The rebuild produced 5889 canonical authors, 6381 aliases, and 7 pending merge candidates; 93 authors are flagged for review. Crossref supplies almost no ORCIDs for AER authors, so OpenAlex and CV evidence remain essential.
- OpenAlex is used only as DOI-keyed enrichment. Its `publication_date` inherits the same one-month shift as Crossref for 2002–2009, so it is stored for comparison but never used to correct issue months. AEA article pages and the AEA all-issues calendar provide the authoritative issue month.
- Citation counts are source-specific: Crossref's `is-referenced-by-count` and OpenAlex's `cited_by_count` are stored separately and not reconciled. The top-cited table reports OpenAlex's values as-is; duplicate works and citation-matching errors can inflate them.
- JEL codes come from AEA article pages. OpenAlex topics are stored separately under `scheme='openalex_topic'`; the two schemes are not treated as equivalent. JEL coverage is partial and not yet uniform by year because the AEA crawler processes in paper order and is resumable; OpenAlex topic coverage is independent and near-complete.
- Abstract provenance is explicit: Crossref, AEA, or OpenAlex. The preferred abstract is kept in `abstract`; the AEA and OpenAlex versions are retained in `abstract_aea` and `abstract_openalex` for audit.
- Institution hints are now resolved against OpenAlex institution IDs. A single authorship may carry several institutions; those links live in `authorship_institutions`. The Crossref byline string remains available in `institution_hint`.
- OpenAlex author links are attached only when a canonical author's authorships map to one OpenAlex ID (or a dominant ID covering at least 80% of them). Multiple IDs, and one ID shared by several canonical authors, are queued as review evidence rather than silently merged or split.
- Retracted originals remain `content_type='article'` with `retraction_status='retracted'` and a pointer to the notice; retraction notices are excluded from research articles. Default views should exclude retracted originals.
