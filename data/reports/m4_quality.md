## Interpretation

- ORCID employment/education records are self-reported and structured. They are used only when an event's start/end interval covers the paper year; no event means no assignment.
- Explicit rank titles (Assistant/Associate/Full Professor, Lecturer, Research Fellow, Postdoc, Emeritus) are high confidence. Generic "Professor" titles are stored as full professor with low confidence because the title may be current rather than the rank at the paper date.
- Doctoral education intervals are used for `phd_student` at medium confidence only when the paper year falls inside the degree interval.
- Byline evidence remains paper-specific and takes precedence over ORCID for the same slot. ORCID fills slots that the byline cannot explain.
- ORCID coverage is not complete: a large share of authors have an ORCID, but many records lack dates or employment entries. CV discovery and parsing are still required for the remaining authors and for rank changes that ORCID does not record.
- ORCID identity is validated before its events are used. A matching AER DOI is the strongest signal; a matching work title or an institution overlap is also accepted. A conservative economics-field signal is accepted only at low confidence. Records with a conflicting person name, a clearly non-economics field, or a career-changing event but no positive evidence are rejected. Records with no positive evidence and no career-changing event remain as low-confidence identity links with events withheld.
- CV events come from public CVs/faculty pages discovered through ORCID researcher URLs. The parser is conservative and section-based; CV assignments are used only after byline and ORCID evidence, and raw CVs are stored only in the local ignored cache.
